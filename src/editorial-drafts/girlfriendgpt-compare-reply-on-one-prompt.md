Use one short question in each original scene and decide what a useful answer would contain before reading the replies. Changing the character, story and question together makes it hard to tell which difference mattered. GirlfriendGPT's terms describe interactions with AI companions. A comparison of your fictional prompts is a way to evaluate fit for your scene.

## Decide what the opening needs to accomplish

Imagine two adult characters who work at the same fictional instrument repair shop. You are the customer in both openings. Ask, “How long would you need to inspect this radio?” A useful first reply should acknowledge your role, state the next action and leave you room to decide whether to wait.

If the first reply sets the radio on a workbench and asks for the model, it has given you a concrete next action. If a second reply describes a long history of the repair shop before asking for the model, both answer paths may exist but their pacing differs. Rate the pacing separately from factual consistency.

## Change only the issue you can identify

If one opening confuses the technician with the customer, revise the role statement at the start. If the opening knows who the customer is but postpones the question for three paragraphs, shorten the greeting. Keep the same question for the next check.

Save a brief note such as “kept: correct speaker and next action by the second sentence.” Keep the task fictional and omit private messages and personal identifiers. That gives you a reusable criterion for the next character without claiming all future replies or models behave the same way.
