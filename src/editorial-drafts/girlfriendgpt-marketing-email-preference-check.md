Review the sender and purpose of the message before changing your settings. GirlfriendGPT's privacy notice describes marketing opt-out through preferences or links in promotional messages. A renewal receipt, password reset and promotional offer are different messages. Identify which one you want to stop.

## Confirm the message belongs to the account

Look at the sender and the account listed in a known receipt or account page. A familiar display name is only one clue. If you are unsure about an email, open the account through your usual route and check its current preferences there. Avoid using a link from a message you cannot verify.

In a fictional inbox, a coupon arrives on Monday, a payment confirmation on Tuesday and a sign-in notice on Wednesday. Save the billing confirmation in your records. Review the Monday message when looking for the marketing control. Leave the login notice available for account security.

## Review the next message by date and category

Save the day of the preference change. If another offer arrives, record its sender, subject and received time. A note saying “promotional message after preference change” is easier to investigate than a screenshot of all unread messages. Use the official support route if the offer appears to come from the same service and the preference remains unclear.

Removing an offer from your inbox has little effect on the underlying account. Check billing independently if your real goal is to verify whether a subscription will renew. Keep only the useful record of the marketing change and the next observed message. That gives support a specific event to review.
